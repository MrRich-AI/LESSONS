CWriting: Review

A review is your evaluation of something you have read or seen, such as a film, TV show, video game or book. A review could also focus on somewhere you have been – for example, a festival, concert or restaurant – or even something that you have used, such as computer software or equipment.

Before writing

When you write a review, you need to make a judgement about something and explain your assessment of it. There are different ways to write a review, but we are going to follow this four-step plan:

1 Introduction
Hook the reader
2 Brief description
What, where, when
3 Opinion
Your views on each feature
4 Recommendation
Should people go?
CWriting: Review

Look at the following task and decide:

  • what you need to include in your review
  • who is going to read it.

You recently visited a new fast-food restaurant in your town with some friends. Your teacher has asked you to write a review of the restaurant for the school magazine.

In your review, you should give your view of the restaurant and say whether you recommend it and why.

Opposite are some comments from the friends who went with you.

Now write a review for the school magazine.

The comments may give you some ideas, and you should also use some ideas of your own.

The staff were friendly and helpful.
I was disappointed by the menu.

WRITING TIP

In any writing task, it is important to understand who your audience is. This will help you to decide if your writing should be informal, neutral or formal.
For this task:
What do you need to include?
Your views about a local fast-food restaurant: give your view of it and say whether you recommend it and why.
Who is going to read it?
People who read the school magazine (other students, teachers, parents), so the style should be neutral or formal.

WRITING TIP

In formal writing tasks, you need to be aware of both the content and the language you choose. When planning your writing, consider the following carefully.

First, consider whether you have stated your ideas clearly, then developed and explained each of them, and also whether you have addressed all the points in the task. Then think about language – consider grammar, spelling and punctuation, vocabulary and varied sentence structures. Finally, ask yourself whether the language and style of your response are appropriate for the audience and purpose of the task.

11Now draft your review. When you have finished, evaluate your draft by asking these questions.
  • Is my answer relevant?
  • Are the purpose, intended audience and level of formality clear?
  • Have I organised the text clearly and logically?
  • Are my ideas well developed and explained?
  • Have I used a range of vocabulary and grammatical structures?
  • Are my spelling, grammar and punctuation accurate?

LANGUAGE FOCUS: Linking devices

Linking devices are words or phrases that are used to make connections between sentences. They have several different functions, including:

  • to sequence events or ideas in a piece of writing
  • to signal a contrast or comparison
  • to indicate cause and effect.

👁LANGUAGE TIP: However and although

Some linking devices have similar meanings, but they cannot always replace each other directly. However and Although are often used incorrectly. Look at these examples to help you avoid making similar mistakes.

✗ Incorrect: Although, whether you want it or not, there is a new place selling fast food.
✓ Correct: However, whether you want it or not, there is a new place selling fast food.
✗ Incorrect: However the outside of the restaurant was ugly, I was pleasantly surprised by the inside.
✓ Correct: Although the outside of the restaurant was ugly, I was pleasantly surprised by the inside.
